## Choosing an integration

Most people start by collecting payments online. There are three ways to do it, and the right one depends on how much control you need over the payment experience.

| | [Simple Checkout](/online-payments/integrations/simple-checkout) | [Standard Checkout](/online-payments/integrations/standard-checkout) | [E-commerce plugin](/online-payments/integrations/e-commerce-plugin) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **How it works** | A payment form opens over your page | You redirect to a SeerBit-hosted page | Install into your store platform |
| **You need** | A public key and a few lines of JavaScript | A server that can make API calls | An account on the platform |
| **Best when** | You want the customer to stay on your site | You want no card handling in your frontend at all | You run WooCommerce, Wix, Magento or similar |
| **Effort** | Lowest for a web page | Low, and works from any language | None — configuration only |

All three are PCI compliant and support every [payment method](/payment-methods/overview) on your account. You can change your mind later; they use the same keys and the same transaction records.

## How an integration fits together

Whichever path you pick, a payment follows the same shape:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Authenticate">
    Exchange your keys for a token, or sign the request — see [Authentication](/authentication).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the transaction">
    Tell SeerBit the amount, the currency and who is paying.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let the customer pay">
    They complete the payment on a SeerBit page, an inline form, or a terminal.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm the result">
    Read the outcome from a [webhook](/online-payments/after-payments/webhook-events) or by [verifying the payment](/online-payments/after-payments/verify-payment) — never from the browser redirect alone.
  </Step>
</Steps>

That last step is the one people skip, and it is the one that causes unfulfilled orders and double shipments. Build it in from the start.
